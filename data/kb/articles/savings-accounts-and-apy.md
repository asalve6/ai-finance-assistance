When banks advertise savings accounts, you will often see the letters **APY**. They are important for comparing accounts.

## What APY means

APY stands for **annual percentage yield**. It tells you how much interest you would earn in one year, as a percent of your money. It includes the effect of compounding, which means earning interest on your interest.

## Rate vs APY

A bank may pay interest every month, or even every day, instead of once a year. Each time it pays, the new interest starts earning interest too. So by the end of the year, you earn a little more than the plain interest rate.

For example, an account that pays 2% a year, added monthly, has an APY of about 2.02%. The difference is small for one year, but it adds up over time.

## Why APY helps

Different banks may add interest at different times. APY puts them all on the same scale. In the US, banks must show the APY for savings accounts, so people can compare them fairly.

## What else to check

- **Fees.** A monthly fee can eat up your interest.
- **Minimum balance.** Some accounts need a certain amount to earn interest or avoid fees.
- **Insurance.** Make sure the bank is FDIC insured, or the credit union is NCUA insured.

## Rates change

Most savings account rates can change over time. A high rate today might be lower next year.

## Try it

With a grown-up, look up the APY on two savings accounts. Which one pays more? Then work out roughly how much interest $100 would earn in each over a year.
