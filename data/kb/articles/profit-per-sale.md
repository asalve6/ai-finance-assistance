Selling more is great, but what really matters is how much you keep from each sale. That is your profit per sale.

## Profit per item

Profit per item = price − cost to make one.

If a cup of lemonade costs 20 cents to make and you sell it for 75 cents, you keep 55 cents per cup.

## Profit margin

The **profit margin** shows that as a percent of the price:

Margin = profit per item ÷ price × 100

For the lemonade: 55 ÷ 75 × 100 ≈ 73%. So about 73 cents of every dollar of sales is profit, before fixed costs.

A high margin means you keep a lot from each sale. A low margin means you need to sell many to earn much.

## Markup is different

**Markup** compares the profit to the cost, not the price. Lemonade with a 20-cent cost and a 55-cent profit has a markup of 55 ÷ 20 × 100 = 275%. Markup and margin describe the same sale in two ways, so check which one someone means.

## Don't forget fixed costs

Things like a sign or a table cost the same no matter how many you sell. Profit per item has to cover those too.

## Breaking even

The **break-even point** is how many you must sell to cover fixed costs. If your sign cost $2.20 and you keep 55 cents a cup:

$2.20 ÷ $0.55 = 4 cups

After 4 cups, every extra cup adds real profit.

In Penny's Lemonade Stand game, check the cost per cup. Pick a price and work out your profit per cup and your margin. Then work out how many cups you'd need to sell to pay back a $3 sign.
