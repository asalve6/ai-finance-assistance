Many grown-ups save for retirement through their job using a **401(k)**. The odd name comes from a section of the US tax law.

## How it works

1. A worker signs up through their employer.
2. They choose a part of each paycheck to save, like 5%.
3. That money goes into their 401(k) account automatically, before they ever see it.
4. Inside the account, the money is usually invested in funds the plan offers. Its value can go up and down.

## The tax benefit

In a **traditional 401(k)**, the money goes in before income tax is taken out. That lowers the worker's taxes today. They pay income tax later, when they take the money out in retirement.

Many plans also offer a **Roth 401(k)**. There, the money goes in after tax. Later, qualified withdrawals in retirement are tax-free.

## The employer match

Some employers add money too. This is called a **match**. For example, an employer might add 50 cents for every dollar a worker saves, up to a limit. A match is extra money for retirement. Many people try to save at least enough to get the whole match.

## Rules to know

- There is a yearly limit on how much a worker can put in. It changes over time, so check the IRS website.
- Taking money out early, usually before age 59 and a half, often means paying income tax plus an extra 10% tax, with some exceptions.
- Some employer match money only becomes fully yours after you work there for a while.

## Why it helps

Saving happens automatically, so it's easy to keep going. Over many years, steady saving and compound growth can add up.

This is for learning, not tax or investment advice.

## Try it

Say a worker earns $1,000 in a paycheck and saves 5% in a 401(k). Their employer matches 50% of what they save. How much goes in from the worker? From the employer? In total?
