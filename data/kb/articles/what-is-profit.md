When you sell something, the money you get is not all yours to keep. First you have to pay for what it cost you. What is left over is called **profit**.

## A simple example

Say you sell bracelets. The beads and string for one bracelet cost $1. You sell each bracelet for $3.

- Money in from one sale: $3
- Cost of making it: $1
- Profit: $3 minus $1 equals $2

So you keep $2 from each bracelet.

## Money in and money out

The money you get from selling is called **revenue**. The money you spend to make and sell things is called **costs**.

Profit = revenue minus costs.

If you sell 10 bracelets, your revenue is $30. Your costs are $10. Your profit is $20.

## What is a loss?

Sometimes costs are bigger than revenue. Then there is no profit. Instead, there is a **loss**.

Say you bought supplies for 10 bracelets, which cost $10, but you only sold 2. You took in $6. That is $4 less than you spent. That is a loss of $4.

A loss is not the end of the world. It is a lesson. Maybe the price was too high, or not enough people knew about your bracelets. You can change one thing and try again.

## Don't forget hidden costs

Some costs are easy to miss. A sign for your stand, bags for the bracelets, or the time you spend all count. Smart sellers write down every cost so their profit number is honest.

## Try it

Think of something you could make and sell, like cookies or drawings. Write down what it would cost to make one, and what price you might charge. Work out the profit on one sale, and on ten sales.
