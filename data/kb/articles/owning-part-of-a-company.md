When you own a share of a company, you are a part owner. It might be a very tiny part, but it is real ownership. What does that actually mean?

## A slice of the whole

Imagine a company is divided into a million equal pieces. Each piece is one share. If you own one share, you own one millionth of the company. If you own 1,000 shares, you own one thousandth.

## What owners get

- **A share of the company's future.** If the company grows and becomes more valuable, each share may become worth more.
- **Possible dividends.** Some companies share profits with owners.
- **A voice.** Shareholders can usually vote on some big decisions, like who sits on the company's board. Usually, one share means one vote.

## What owners don't get

Owning a share of a game company does not mean free games. You can't walk into the office and give orders. The company is run by its leaders and its board of directors. Shareholders help choose the board.

## The risk of owning

Owners share in the bad times too. If the company struggles, the share price can fall. If a company fails completely, its shares can become worth nothing. The most an owner usually loses, though, is what they paid for the shares.

## Owners and lenders

Lending money to a company is different from owning it. A lender, like someone holding a bond, expects to be paid back with interest. An owner has no promised payment. Owners take more risk, in exchange for a share of the possible rewards.

This article is for learning. It is not advice about what to buy.
