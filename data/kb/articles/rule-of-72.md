How long would it take for money to double? You could use a calculator. Or you could use a quick shortcut called the **rule of 72**.

## The rule

Divide 72 by the yearly growth rate. The answer is about how many years it takes to double.

Years to double ≈ 72 ÷ rate

## Examples

- At 6% a year: 72 ÷ 6 = 12. Money doubles in about 12 years.
- At 3% a year: 72 ÷ 3 = 24. About 24 years.
- At 9% a year: 72 ÷ 9 = 8. About 8 years.

So a higher rate doubles money faster. Doubling the rate roughly cuts the time in half.

## Why does it work?

It comes from the math of compound growth, where money earns interest on its interest. The number 72 is handy because it divides evenly by many numbers, like 2, 3, 4, 6, 8, 9, and 12. The rule is an estimate. It works best for rates between about 4% and 12%.

## The rule works on costs too

If prices rise 3% a year because of inflation, things cost about twice as much in about 24 years. If a debt grows at 18% a year, it doubles in about 4 years. That shows why high interest on debt is so costly.

## Important limits

The rule assumes the same rate every year. Real savings rates change. Investments like stocks do not grow at a steady rate at all. Some years they go up, some years they go down. So the rule is a thinking tool, not a promise.

## Try it

Use the rule of 72 to estimate how long money takes to double at 4%, 8%, and 12%. Then use the compound interest calculator on Investor.gov to check how close your estimates were.
