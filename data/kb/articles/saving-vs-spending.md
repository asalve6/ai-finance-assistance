Every time you have money, you face a choice. Spend it now, or save it for later? Neither choice is always right. The trick is to choose on purpose.

## Spending is not bad

Spending money on things you need, or things that make you happy, is part of life. Money is a tool, and spending is one way to use it. The goal is not to never spend. It is to spend on what matters most to you.

## Saving gives you choices later

Saved money gives you more options in the future. It can help you buy something bigger, handle a surprise, or help someone else. Saving now means you have more choices later.

## Questions to ask

Before you spend, ask:

- **Do I need it, or do I want it?** Needs come first.
- **Will I still care about this next week?** If not, maybe wait.
- **What else could this money do?** Every dollar spent here can't be used for your goal.
- **Can I get it for less?** Maybe somewhere else, or later on sale.

## Giving up one thing for another

When you choose one thing, you give up another. If you spend $5 on a snack, that $5 can't go toward your game. Economists call the thing you give up the **opportunity cost**. Thinking about it helps you make better choices.

## Find a balance

Many people split their money: some to save, some to spend, and some to give. That way you enjoy money today and still build for tomorrow.

## Try it

Next time you want to buy something, stop and ask the four questions above. Write down your answers. Then decide. Did the questions change what you chose?
