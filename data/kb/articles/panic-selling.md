When prices fall fast, people can feel scared and want to get out right away. Selling quickly because of fear is called **panic selling**.

## Why it happens

Losing money feels bad. Studies of how people make choices show that a loss often feels worse than an equal gain feels good. When prices drop and the news is full of alarming headlines, it is natural to want to stop the pain by selling.

## How it can hurt

- **It locks in the loss.** If you paid $100 for something now worth $70, the loss is only on paper while you hold it. Selling makes the $30 loss real.
- **It can miss a recovery.** Prices sometimes bounce back after a drop. Someone who sold at the bottom isn't there for the rebound.
- **Buying back is hard.** After selling, people often wait for prices to feel "safe" again, and may buy back higher than they sold.

Prices don't always recover, though. Sometimes a company truly is in trouble. That is why decisions should come from careful thinking, not fear.

## Ways people avoid it

- **Have a plan before the drop.** Decide ahead of time what you are investing for and how long you will wait.
- **Keep money you need soon somewhere steady,** so a drop doesn't force you to sell.
- **Spread money out,** so one company's fall doesn't sink everything.
- **Pause.** Waiting a day before a big decision, like the 24-hour rule for shopping, helps feelings settle.

## Practice helps

A practice portfolio is a safe way to feel what a drop is like without real money at stake.

This article is for learning. It is not advice about what to buy or sell.

## Try it

Look at your Penny practice portfolio on a day when it goes down. Write down how it makes you feel and what you would want to do. Then wait a week and look again. Did your feelings change?
