Imagine carrying all your eggs in one basket. If you trip, every egg could break. If you split them into a few baskets, one fall breaks only some. That is the idea behind **diversification**.

## What it means with money

Diversification means spreading your money across different things instead of putting it all in one place.

If all your money is in one company's shares, that company's ups and downs matter a lot. If it has a bad year, your whole portfolio feels it. When money is spread across several companies, one company's bad day hurts the total less.

## Concentrated or spread out?

- A portfolio is **concentrated** when most of the money is in one company.
- It is **spread out** when the money is shared more evenly across several companies.

Penny's Portfolio tab shows how spread out a practice portfolio is. It also shows the biggest share, so you can see if one company is taking up most of it.

## Different kinds of companies

Spreading money across companies that do different things can help even more. A game company and a food company may not have bad days at the same time. People also spread money across different kinds of investments, not just shares.

## What it can't do

Diversification lowers the risk that one company sinks everything. It does not remove all risk. Sometimes many prices fall at the same time, like when the whole economy has a hard year. Spreading out also means one star company can't lift your whole portfolio as much.

This article is for learning. It is not advice about what to buy.
