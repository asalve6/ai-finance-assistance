Some information is like a key to your money. If the wrong person gets it, they could use it to take money or pretend to be you. Keeping that information private is an important money skill.

## What to keep private

- **Passwords** for games, apps, and email
- **PINs**, the secret numbers for cards
- **Codes** sent to a phone to log in
- **Card numbers** and bank account numbers
- **Your full name, address, school, and birthday** together
- **Family money details**, like how much grown-ups earn

## Where risks hide

- **Messages from strangers.** Someone you don't know asks for details, even if they seem friendly.
- **Fake websites.** A site that looks real but has a strange address.
- **Game chats.** Offers of free coins or items in exchange for your login.
- **Public posts.** Photos or posts that show your address, school, or a card.

## Simple safety habits

1. **Never share passwords**, not even with friends. Grown-ups at home can help you keep them safe.
2. **Use strong passwords**: long, and different for each account.
3. **Ask before you type.** Before entering any personal information online, ask a trusted grown-up.
4. **Look for the lock.** Real shopping and banking sites use secure connections, often shown by a lock icon. Even then, check with a grown-up.
5. **Tell someone.** If something feels wrong, stop and tell a grown-up. You won't be in trouble.

## Penny keeps it simple

Penny only asks for a nickname, never your real name, and never asks about your family's money.

## Try it

With a grown-up, make a list of your accounts, like games or apps. For each one, check that the password is strong and not shared. Then talk about one thing you'd do if a stranger asked for your information.
