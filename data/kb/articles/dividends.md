When a company makes a profit, it has choices. It can use the money to grow, save it, or share some of it with its owners. When it shares money with shareholders, that payment is called a **dividend**.

## How dividends work

Dividends are usually paid per share. If a company pays a dividend of 50 cents per share and you own 10 shares, you get $5. Many US companies that pay dividends do it four times a year, but some pay once a year or at other times.

## Not every company pays

Many companies, especially young ones growing fast, pay no dividend. They put their profits back into the business, hoping to grow bigger. Older, steady companies are more likely to pay dividends. Neither type is better. They are just different.

## Dividends are not promised

A company decides whether to pay a dividend, and how much. It can raise it, lower it, or stop it, especially in hard times. So a dividend paid in the past is no promise of one in the future.

## Dividend yield

People often compare dividends using the **dividend yield**. It is the yearly dividend divided by the share price, shown as a percent.

For example, if a share costs $50 and pays $2 a year in dividends, the yield is $2 ÷ $50 = 4%.

When the share price changes, the yield changes too, even if the dividend stays the same.

## Two ways to gain

Shareholders can gain in two ways: from dividends, and from the share price rising. But the price can also fall, and that can wipe out more than the dividend paid.

## Reinvesting

Some people use their dividends to buy more shares. That is called reinvesting, and it is a form of compound growth.

This article is for learning. It is not advice about what to buy.
