The cereal you eat, the shoes you wear, and the games you play were all made by companies. But what is a company, exactly?

## A business with a job

A **company** is a business. It makes something, like toys or snacks, or does something, like fixing cars or making movies. Then it sells those things to **customers**.

## The people in a company

- **Owners** started the company or bought a piece of it. If the company does well, the owners can do well too.
- **Employees** are the people who work there. They get paid for their work.
- **Customers** are the people who buy what the company sells.

A small company might have one owner who does everything. A big company might have thousands of employees and many owners.

## How does a company make money?

A company earns money when customers buy its products. It also spends money on things like materials, rent, and paying workers. If it earns more than it spends, it makes a **profit**. If it spends more than it earns, it has a **loss**.

## Small and big

Your lemonade stand is a tiny business. You are the owner and the worker, and your neighbors are the customers. Big companies work the same way, just with many more people and much more money.

## Who owns big companies?

Some big companies are owned by just a few people. Others sell small pieces of themselves, called **shares**, to the public. People who own shares are part owners of the company.

## Try it

Look around your room and pick three things. For each one, find out which company made it. What does each company sell? Who are its customers? Do you think it has many employees or just a few?
