If you watch a company's share price, you will see it change all day long. Why does it keep moving?

## Buyers and sellers

A share price is set by people buying and selling. If more people want to buy a share than sell it, buyers offer more, and the price tends to rise. If more people want to sell, sellers accept less, and the price tends to fall. It is supply and demand.

## What changes people's minds?

People buy or sell based on what they think a company is worth. Many things can change that.

- **Company results.** Companies report how much they earned. Better results than expected can push the price up. Worse results can push it down.
- **News.** A new product, a problem with a product, or a new leader can all move the price.
- **The whole economy.** When many businesses are doing well, many prices may rise together. When times are hard, many may fall.
- **Feelings.** Sometimes prices move because people feel excited or scared, even without big news.

## One day does not tell you much

A price can go up today and down tomorrow. A single day's move is often just noise. Looking at a longer time, like months or years, gives a better picture.

## Nobody knows what comes next

Even experts cannot know for sure where a price is headed. Anyone who says they know is guessing. That is why stocks are called risky. The price you sell at could be higher or lower than the price you paid.

This article is for learning. It is not advice about what to buy or sell.
