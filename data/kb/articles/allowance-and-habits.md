An allowance is money a grown-up gives you on a regular schedule, like every week. Some families tie it to chores. Others give it so kids can practice managing money. Either way, it is your chance to build good habits.

## Why habits matter

A habit is something you do so often that you do it without thinking. Brushing your teeth is a habit. Money habits work the same way. If you save a little every time you get money, it soon feels normal.

## Pay yourself first

One strong habit is to **pay yourself first**. As soon as you get your allowance, put your savings part away before you spend anything. If you wait until the end of the week, there is often nothing left to save.

## A simple weekly routine

1. Get your allowance.
2. Put your saving part in the jar right away.
3. Set aside any money you plan to give.
4. Keep the rest for spending this week.

Doing the same steps each week turns them into a habit.

## Make your own choices

An allowance lets you make real choices, and sometimes mistakes. Maybe you spend it all on day one and wish you had waited. That is okay. It is much better to learn this lesson with a small allowance now than with a big paycheck later.

## Talk about it

Ask the grown-up who gives your allowance how they learned about money. Their stories can teach you a lot.

## Try it

For the next four weeks, write down your allowance and what you did with it: saved, spent, or gave. At the end, look at your notes. Did you pay yourself first each week? What would you change?
