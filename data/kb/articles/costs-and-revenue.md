Every business, big or small, keeps track of two things: money coming in and money going out.

## Revenue: money coming in

**Revenue** is all the money a business gets from selling. If you sell 12 cups of lemonade for 75 cents each, your revenue is 12 times 75 cents, which is $9.

## Costs: money going out

**Costs** are what the business spends to make and sell its things. There are two kinds.

- **Fixed costs** stay the same no matter how much you sell. A sign for your stand or a pitcher is a fixed cost. You pay for it once, whether you sell 2 cups or 50.
- **Variable costs** go up when you make more. Lemons, sugar, and cups are variable costs. More cups of lemonade means more lemons.

## Putting them together

Profit = revenue minus costs.

Say your sign cost $2, and each cup costs 20 cents to make. You make and sell 12 cups.

- Revenue: $9
- Variable costs: 12 times 20 cents is $2.40
- Fixed costs: $2
- Total costs: $4.40
- Profit: $9 minus $4.40 equals $4.60

## Why fixed costs matter

On a slow day, you still paid for the sign. That is why selling more can help. Each extra cup spreads the fixed cost over more sales.

## Watch for waste

Cups you make but don't sell still cost money. They add to costs without adding revenue. Making the right amount is part of running a business well.
