A **scam** is a trick. Someone pretends to be helpful, or offers something amazing, so they can take your money or your private information. Scammers are good at sounding real. Knowing the warning signs keeps you safe.

## Warning signs

- **You "won" something you didn't enter.** "Congratulations, you won a free phone! Just pay a small fee." Real prizes don't ask you to pay.
- **They rush you.** "Act now or lose it forever!" Scammers want you to decide before you think.
- **They ask for passwords or codes.** No real company, game, or friend needs your password. Never share it, or the code sent to your phone.
- **They want gift cards.** Asking to be paid with gift cards is a classic scam sign.
- **Free game money or items.** Offers of free coins or skins that ask you to log in on a strange site are often tricks to steal your account.
- **The message feels a bit off.** Odd spelling, a strange web address, or a "friend" acting unlike themselves can all be clues.

## What to do

1. **Stop.** You don't have to answer or click anything.
2. **Don't share.** Keep passwords, codes, and family information private.
3. **Tell a grown-up.** Show them the message. You won't be in trouble for asking.
4. **Check for yourself.** Go to the real website or app yourself instead of clicking a link in a message.

Grown-ups can report scams to the Federal Trade Commission, the FTC. That helps protect other people too.

## Everyone can be fooled

Scammers trick adults as well as kids. Being careful is not about being scared. It is about slowing down and asking, "Does this make sense?"

## Try it

With a grown-up, look at a few messages, emails, or ads together. Find any warning signs from the list above. Make a family rule for what to do when a message seems suspicious.
