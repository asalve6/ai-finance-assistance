A stock quote is a quick report card for a company's shares. It packs a lot of information into a small space. Here is how to read one.

## The ticker symbol

Every company that sells shares to the public has a short code called a **ticker symbol**. For example, Walt Disney uses DIS, and Coca-Cola uses KO. Tickers help people find the right company fast.

## The price

The big number is the **last price**. That is the price of one share the last time someone bought or sold it. If the market is closed, it is the price at the end of the last trading day.

## The change

Next to the price, you will see how much it moved since the **previous close**. The previous close is the price at the end of the last trading day.

The change can be shown two ways:

- In dollars, like down $0.09
- As a percent, like down 0.08%

Green or a plus sign usually means up. Red or a minus sign usually means down.

## Other numbers you might see

- **Open:** the price when trading started today.
- **Day's range:** the lowest and highest prices today.
- **Volume:** how many shares were traded today.
- **52-week range:** the lowest and highest prices over the past year.
- **Market cap:** the total value of all the company's shares.

## What a quote does not tell you

A quote shows what happened. It does not show what will happen next. A green number today does not mean the price will keep rising.
