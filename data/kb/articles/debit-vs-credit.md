Debit cards and credit cards look almost the same. You tap, swipe, or insert both to pay. But how they work is very different.

## Debit cards: your own money

A **debit card** is connected to a bank account. When you pay with it, the money comes out of that account, usually right away. You can only spend money that is already there.

Some banks offer debit cards for teens, with a parent's account linked so they can help.

## Credit cards: borrowed money

A **credit card** lets you borrow money from the card company to pay for things. Later, you get a bill. If you pay the whole bill on time, you usually pay no extra.

If you don't pay it all, the company charges **interest** on what is left. Credit card interest rates are often high, so a small purchase can end up costing much more over time.

## An example

Say you buy a $100 jacket with a credit card and pay only a small part each month. With interest, you could end up paying well over $100, and it could take a long time to pay off.

## Credit scores

When people use credit, companies keep track of whether they pay on time. This record creates a **credit score**. A good score can make it easier and cheaper to borrow later, for a car or a home. Paying late can hurt it.

## Staying safe with cards

- Keep your card and its PIN secret.
- Check your account for purchases you don't recognize.
- Tell a grown-up and the bank right away if a card is lost.

## Try it

Ask a grown-up to show you a bank statement and a credit card bill, with private numbers covered. Find a purchase on each. Talk about how the money moved differently in each case.
