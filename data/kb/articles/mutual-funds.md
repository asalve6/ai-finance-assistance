A **mutual fund** is a way for many people to invest together. Everyone puts money into one big pool, and the fund invests the pool in many different things.

## Pooling money

Imagine ten friends who each have $10. Alone, each could buy only a little. Together they have $100 and can buy a wider mix. A mutual fund works the same way, but with thousands of people and much more money. Each person owns a share of the whole pool.

## Who decides what to buy?

- In an **actively managed** fund, a professional called a **fund manager** picks investments, trying to do better than the market.
- In an **index fund**, the fund simply copies an index, like a list of large companies.

## The price: once a day

A mutual fund's share price is called its **net asset value**, or NAV. It is worked out once a day, after the stock market closes, from the value of everything the fund owns. If you buy or sell during the day, you get that end-of-day price. That is one difference from an ETF, which trades all day.

## Fees

Running a fund costs money, so funds charge fees. The yearly fee is called the **expense ratio**. Actively managed funds usually charge more than index funds. Over many years, even a small difference in fees can add up.

## Risks

A mutual fund that holds stocks goes up and down with those stocks. It is not insured like a bank account, and you can get back less than you put in.

This article is for learning. It is not advice about what to buy.

## Try it

Imagine 4 friends pool $25 each into a "club fund." It buys things now worth $120 in total. What is each friend's share worth? What if the total drops to $80?
