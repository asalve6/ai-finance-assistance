A piggy bank is a great start. But as your savings grow, a real bank can be a safer home for your money.

## What is a bank?

A bank is a business that keeps money for people. When you put money in, that is a **deposit**. When you take money out, that is a **withdrawal**. The bank keeps a record of every deposit and withdrawal in your **account**.

## Why use a bank?

- **Safety.** Money at home can get lost or stolen. Money in a bank is kept safe.
- **Interest.** Many savings accounts pay you a little extra money, called interest, for keeping your money there.
- **Records.** You can see how much you have and how it changed.

## Is my money safe in a bank?

In the US, most banks are insured by the FDIC. That is a government agency. If an insured bank fails, the FDIC protects your deposits. The standard amount is $250,000 per depositor, per bank, for each type of account ownership. That is far more than most people keep in one bank.

## Kinds of accounts

- A **savings account** is for money you want to keep. It often pays interest.
- A **checking account** is for money you plan to spend. You can use a debit card or pay bills from it.

Kids usually open an account with a parent or guardian. The grown-up helps manage it until the child is older.

## How does a bank pay interest?

Banks lend some of the money people deposit to others, for things like homes and cars. The borrowers pay the bank extra for the loan. The bank shares a small part of that with savers as interest.

## Try it

Ask a grown-up to show you a bank statement or a banking app. Find the deposits, the withdrawals, and the balance. If there is a line for interest, see how much it was this month.
