Sometimes people need money they don't have yet. They can borrow it. Borrowing is useful, but it comes with a cost and a promise.

## What is debt?

When you borrow money, you promise to pay it back. The amount you still owe is called **debt**. A **loan** is borrowed money with set rules for paying it back.

## The cost of borrowing

Most lenders charge **interest**. That is extra money you pay for using their money. The longer you take to pay back, the more interest you usually pay.

Here is a simple example. You borrow $100 and must pay 10% interest after a year. You pay back $110. The $10 is the cost of borrowing.

## Why people borrow

Some things cost more than most people can save quickly:

- A home, often paid for with a loan called a mortgage
- A car
- College

Borrowing can make these possible. Grown-ups think carefully about whether they can make the payments.

## When debt becomes a problem

Debt can grow if payments are missed, because interest keeps adding up. High-interest debt, like an unpaid credit card bill, can grow fast. Owing too much can make it hard to pay for everyday things.

## Smart borrowing questions

- Do I really need this now, or can I save for it?
- How much will it cost in total, with interest?
- Can I make the payments every time?

## Borrowing from friends

Even borrowing $5 from a friend is a promise. Paying it back on time keeps the friendship strong and builds trust.

## Try it

Imagine you want a $60 game. Option one: save $10 a week for 6 weeks. Option two: borrow $60 now and pay back $70 over 7 weeks. Which costs more? Which would you choose, and why?
