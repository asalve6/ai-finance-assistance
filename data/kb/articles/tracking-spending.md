Have you ever looked in your wallet and wondered, "Where did all my money go?" Tracking your spending answers that question.

## What is tracking?

Tracking means writing down every time you spend money. You note what you bought, how much it cost, and the date. It sounds simple, and it is. But it can surprise you.

## Why track?

Small amounts are easy to forget. A snack here, a game item there. Each one feels tiny. But when you add them up at the end of the week or month, the total can be bigger than you expected.

Tracking shows you the truth about your habits. Once you know, you can decide whether to change anything.

## How to track

Pick a way that is easy for you:

- A small notebook you carry with you
- A page on the fridge at home
- A notes app, with a grown-up's help

Write each purchase right away, before you forget.

## Look for patterns

After a week or two, look over your list. Group things into types, like food, games, and gifts. Ask:

- Which type did I spend the most on?
- Was any of it an impulse buy?
- Is there something I would rather spend that money on?

## Tracking helps your budget

A budget is a plan for your money. Tracking shows what really happened. Comparing the two tells you if your plan is working.

## No judging

Tracking is not about feeling bad. It is about knowing. Even grown-ups are often surprised when they start tracking.

## Try it

For two weeks, write down every purchase you make. At the end, add them up by type. Share what you found with a grown-up. Was there anything you did not expect?
