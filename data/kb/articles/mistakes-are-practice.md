Everyone makes money mistakes. Grown-ups do, and so do kids. The good news is that mistakes are one of the best ways to learn.

## Small mistakes, big lessons

Maybe you spent your whole allowance on the first day and had nothing left for a movie with friends. Or you bought a toy that broke right away. Those moments don't feel great. But they teach lessons you will remember.

Making these mistakes now, with small amounts, is much better than making them later with a paycheck or a loan.

## Turn a mistake into a lesson

When something goes wrong with money, ask:

1. **What happened?** Describe it without blaming yourself.
2. **Why did it happen?** Was it a rushed choice, a missing plan, or bad luck?
3. **What will I try next time?** Pick one small change.

## Practice is safe

That is why games and pretend money are so useful. In Penny's Piggy Bank and Lemonade Stand games, and in the practice portfolio, you can try things and see what happens without losing real money. A bad lemonade day is a lesson, not a disaster.

## Be kind to yourself

Feeling upset about a mistake is normal. But don't stay stuck. Even famous business leaders made plenty of mistakes before they succeeded. What matters is what you do next.

## Talk about it

Ask a grown-up about a money mistake they made and what they learned. Most people have a story. Sharing mistakes helps everyone learn.

## Try it

Think of a time something didn't go the way you wanted with money, or in a Penny game. Answer the three questions above. Write down one thing you'll try differently next time.
