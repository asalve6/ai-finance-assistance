Have you ever had exactly enough money for something, then found out at the checkout that it costs more? That surprise is usually **sales tax**.

## What is sales tax?

Sales tax is a small extra amount added to the price of many things you buy. The store collects it from you and sends it to the government.

The price on the tag usually does not include sales tax in the US. The tax is added when you pay.

## How much is it?

Sales tax is a percent of the price. The rate depends on where you live. States set their own rates, and many cities and counties add a little more. A few states have no statewide sales tax at all.

Here is an example. If a toy costs $10.00 and the sales tax rate is 6%, the tax is 60 cents. You pay $10.60.

## What is it for?

Sales tax helps pay for things a community shares. That can include schools, roads, parks, police, and fire departments. Each place decides how to use its tax money.

## What gets taxed?

It depends on the state. Some states don't charge sales tax on certain things, like some foods or medicine. Others tax almost everything. That is why the tax on two receipts might look different.

## Plan for it

When you save for something, remember to add a little for tax. If a game costs $40 and your sales tax is about 7%, plan for about $42.80. That way you won't come up short at the register.

This is for learning, not tax advice.

## Try it

Look at a store receipt at home. Find the line that shows tax. Divide the tax by the total before tax, then multiply by 100. That is the sales tax rate where that store is.
