A lemonade stand is a tiny business. Running one teaches the same ideas that big companies use every day.

## Start with your costs

Before you sell a single cup, you spend money. You need lemons, sugar, cups, and ice. Those are your **costs**.

Work out the cost of one cup. If your supplies cost $4 and make 20 cups, each cup costs 20 cents to make.

## Choose a price

Your price should be higher than your cost, or you will not make a profit. If a cup costs 20 cents to make and you sell it for 75 cents, you keep 55 cents per cup.

But a very high price can scare customers away. A very low price might bring lots of customers, but you keep less from each cup. Finding the right price takes some testing.

## Watch the weather

On a hot, sunny day, lots of people want a cold drink. On a cloudy or rainy day, fewer people stop. Smart sellers make more cups on hot days and fewer on cool days. That way they don't waste lemonade.

## Too many or too few cups

- If you make too many cups, the extra ones may go to waste. You paid for them but did not sell them.
- If you make too few, you run out while customers are still waiting. You miss sales.

Guessing how many people will come is part of the job. Each day you learn a little more.

## Keep a record

Write down how many cups you made, how many you sold, the weather, and your profit. After a few days, you will see patterns. That helps you plan better.

## Try it

Play the Lemonade Stand game in Penny. Try a sunny day and a rainy day with the same price. How many cups did you sell each time? Then change only the price and see what happens to your profit.
