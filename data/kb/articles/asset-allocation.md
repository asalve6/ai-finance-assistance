Diversification means not putting all your eggs in one basket. **Asset allocation** goes one step further. It decides what kinds of baskets you use, and how many eggs go in each.

## The main kinds of assets

- **Stocks:** pieces of companies. They can grow more over time, but they swing up and down the most.
- **Bonds:** loans to governments or companies. They usually swing less than stocks and pay interest, but they tend to grow less.
- **Cash:** savings accounts and similar. Very steady and easy to reach, but they grow slowly and can fall behind rising prices.

Asset allocation is choosing the mix, for example 60% stocks, 30% bonds, and 10% cash.

## Why the mix matters

Different kinds of assets often behave differently at the same time. When stocks have a bad year, bonds or cash may hold steadier. A mix can smooth out the bumps.

## Time and goals

How soon you need the money shapes the mix.

- Money needed soon, like for next year, usually belongs in steadier places, because there is little time to recover from a drop.
- Money not needed for many years can usually ride out more ups and downs.

People also think about how they would feel during a big drop. A mix you can stick with matters.

## Rebalancing

Over time, the mix drifts. If stocks rise a lot, they might grow from 60% to 70% of the total. **Rebalancing** means moving money around to get back to the planned mix.

## No perfect answer

There is no single right allocation for everyone. It depends on goals, time, and comfort with risk. Many grown-ups get help from a trusted professional to decide.

This article is for learning. It is not advice about what to buy.

## Try it

Imagine $100 split 60% stocks, 30% bonds, and 10% cash. How many dollars go in each? If the stocks part grows to $80 and the rest stays the same, what percent is stocks now?
