When it comes to growing savings, time is one of your best helpers. That is why people say starting early wins.

## Time and compound growth

With compound interest, you earn interest on your money and also on the interest it already earned. Each year, the pile gets a little bigger, so the next year's interest is a little bigger too. The more years this goes on, the bigger the effect.

## Two savers

Here is a made-up example using a steady 5% a year.

- **Saver A** puts in $100 a year from age 15 to 25, then stops.
- **Saver B** waits, then puts in $100 a year from age 25 to 35, and stops.

They put in the same total amount. But at age 65, Saver A ends up with much more, because their money had ten extra years to grow. Real rates go up and down, so real results will differ. The lesson is about time.

## Small amounts count

You don't need a lot of money to start. A small amount saved each week, kept up over many years, can grow into something meaningful. The habit matters more than the size.

## Starting early with learning too

Starting early is not only about money. Learning how money works while you are young helps you make good choices when you are older. Every article you read and every game you play is a head start.

## A caution

Starting early helps, but it is not magic. Savings accounts pay modest rates, and investments can go down as well as up. Starting early gives you time, and time makes ups and downs easier to handle.

## Try it

Use the compound interest calculator on Investor.gov with a grown-up. Try $10 a month for 10 years, then for 30 years, at the same rate. How much bigger is the 30-year result?
