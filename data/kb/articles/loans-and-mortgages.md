Most people can't pay for a home or a car all at once. So they borrow money with a **loan** and pay it back a little at a time.

## The parts of a loan

- **Principal:** the amount borrowed.
- **Interest rate:** the yearly cost of borrowing, as a percent.
- **Term:** how long you have to pay it back, like 5 years.
- **Monthly payment:** what you pay each month. Part goes to interest, and part pays down the principal.

## Common kinds of loans

- **Car loans** help people buy a car. They usually last a few years.
- **Student loans** help pay for college or job training. Some come from the government, and some from banks.
- **Mortgages** help people buy a home.

## What is a mortgage?

A **mortgage** is a loan for buying a home. Homes cost a lot, so mortgages are big and long. Many last 15 or 30 years.

Buyers usually pay part of the price up front. That is the **down payment**. The mortgage covers the rest.

The home itself backs the loan. If the owner stops paying, the lender can take the home. That is called foreclosure. It is one reason families plan carefully before buying.

## Interest adds up over time

On a long loan, interest can add up to a lot. Over 30 years, a family might pay much more in interest than they expected. A lower rate, a shorter term, or a bigger down payment can lower the total cost.

## Before borrowing

Grown-ups ask questions like:

- Can we make every monthly payment, even if something goes wrong?
- What will the loan cost in total, with interest?
- Are there fees?
- Could we save up and borrow less?

## Try it

Ask a grown-up how they paid for something big, like a car or a home. Did they use a loan? How long did it take to pay back? What did they learn?
