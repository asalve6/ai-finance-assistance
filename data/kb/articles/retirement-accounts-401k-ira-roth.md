Retirement is the time when people stop working, often in their 60s. Money still has to come from somewhere. Retirement accounts are special accounts that help people save for that time, with tax benefits as a reward.

## The 401(k)

A **401(k)** is a retirement plan offered through a job. The name comes from a section of the US tax law.

- The worker chooses to put part of each paycheck into the account.
- In a traditional 401(k), that money goes in before income tax is taken out. The tax is paid later, when the money is taken out in retirement.
- Some employers add extra money, called a **match**. For example, an employer might add 50 cents for each dollar a worker puts in, up to a limit.

## The IRA

An **IRA**, or individual retirement arrangement, is a retirement account a person opens on their own, not through a job. You need income from work to put money into one. A traditional IRA can also lower your taxes now and tax the money later.

## What does Roth mean?

Both 401(k)s and IRAs can come in a **Roth** version. The difference is when you pay tax.

- **Traditional:** no tax now, pay tax later when you take the money out.
- **Roth:** pay tax now, then qualified withdrawals in retirement are tax-free.

A teen with a job can have a Roth IRA, with a parent's help.

## Rules to know

The government sets limits on how much can go into these accounts each year. Those limits change, so check the IRS website for current numbers. Taking money out early, before retirement age, usually means extra taxes or a penalty, with some exceptions.

## Why start early?

Money saved early has more years to grow. That is the power of compound growth over time. Even small amounts can matter.

This is for learning, not tax advice.

## Try it

Ask a grown-up if their job offers a retirement plan, and whether it has a match. Then work out: if someone puts in $100 and the employer matches 50%, how much goes into the account in total?
