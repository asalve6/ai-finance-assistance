Every money choice has two sides. One side is the **reward**, what you might gain. The other side is the **risk**, the chance that things go worse than you hoped.

## The trade-off

In money, risk and reward usually go together.

- Choices with a chance of a bigger reward usually carry more risk.
- Safer choices usually offer a smaller reward.

This is called a **trade-off**. You can't usually get a big reward with no risk at all. If someone promises that, be very careful.

## Two examples

**A savings account** at an insured bank is low risk. Your money is protected, and you earn a little interest. The reward is small, but steady.

**Shares in a company** carry more risk. The price could rise, and you might gain more than a savings account pays. But the price could also fall, and you could get back less than you put in.

Neither choice is good or bad on its own. They fit different goals.

## Time matters

How soon you need the money changes how much risk makes sense. Money for next week's school trip should be somewhere safe and easy to reach. Money that won't be needed for many years can ride out more ups and downs. Even then, nothing is certain.

## Ways people manage risk

- **Spreading money out** across different things, so one bad result hurts less.
- **Keeping an emergency fund** in a safe place for surprises.
- **Learning first,** like practicing with a pretend portfolio.

## Risk in everyday life

You take small risks all the time. Trying out for a team risks not making it, but the reward could be playing a sport you love. Money works the same way.

This article is for learning. It is not advice about what to buy.

## Try it

Think of a choice where you took a small risk, like trying a new food or a new game. What was the possible reward? What could have gone wrong? Now think of a money example and list its risk and reward.
