Every day, people make choices about money. One of the best habits you can build is to ask a simple question first: is this a need or a want?

## What is a need?

A need is something you must have to stay healthy and safe. Food, a place to live, clothes for the weather, and medicine are needs. You cannot go without them.

## What is a want?

A want is something that would be nice to have, but you can live without it. A new video game, a second pair of fancy shoes, or a snack at the mall are wants. Wants are not bad. They make life fun. The trick is to choose them on purpose.

## Some things are in the middle

Sometimes the line is blurry. You need shoes, but you may not need the most expensive pair. You need to eat, but you do not need a meal at a restaurant. When a need has a cheaper choice and a fancier choice, the cheaper one already covers the need. The extra money you pay for the fancy one is the want part.

## Trade-offs

Money is limited, so choosing one thing often means giving up another. This is called a trade-off. If you spend ten dollars on a toy today, you cannot also put that ten dollars toward your bike fund. Neither choice is always right. What matters is that you notice the trade-off before you spend.

## Try it

Look at the last three things you bought or asked for. Label each one as a need or a want. Then pick one want you could wait on for a week. When the week is over, ask yourself if you still want it just as much.
