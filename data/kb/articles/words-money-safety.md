Keeping money safe has its own words. Knowing them helps you spot trouble early.

## PIN

A **PIN**, or personal identification number, is a secret number used with a bank card. Never share it or write it on the card.

## Password

A **password** is a secret word or phrase that protects an account. Strong passwords are long and different for every account.

## Two-factor authentication

**Two-factor authentication** means logging in takes two steps, like a password plus a code sent to a phone. It makes it much harder for someone else to get in.

## Phishing

**Phishing** is when someone sends a fake message, email, or link that looks real, to trick you into giving away passwords or other private details. If a message asks you to log in through a link, check with a grown-up first.

## Scam

A **scam** is any trick to take money or information. Pressure, prizes you didn't enter, and requests for gift cards are warning signs.

## Identity theft

**Identity theft** is when someone uses another person's private details, like their name and numbers, to pretend to be them, often to borrow or spend money.

## Secure website

A **secure website** protects information sent to it. Its address usually starts with "https" and shows a lock. A lock alone doesn't prove the site is honest.

## Report

If something seems wrong, **report** it. Tell a grown-up right away. Grown-ups can report scams and identity theft to the government.

## Try it

Make a "safety word" card with three words from this list and what each means. Keep it near the computer, and use it to quiz someone at home.
