You may have heard of **bitcoin**. It is one kind of **cryptocurrency**, often called crypto. Crypto is digital money that exists only on computers.

## How is it different from dollars?

- **No coins or bills.** Crypto lives in digital accounts called wallets.
- **Not run by a bank.** Most crypto is not issued or backed by a government the way US dollars are.
- **A shared record.** Many cryptocurrencies keep a public list of every transaction, called a **blockchain**. Many computers around the world keep copies of it.

## Prices swing wildly

Crypto prices can rise or fall by large amounts, sometimes in a single day. Some people have made money. Many others have lost a lot. Prices often move on hype and fear rather than on a business earning profits. This makes crypto very risky.

## Not protected like a bank

Money in an insured bank account is protected by the FDIC if the bank fails. Crypto usually has no such protection. If a crypto company fails, or a wallet is hacked, the money can be gone.

## Payments can't be undone

With a credit card, you can often dispute a wrong charge. Crypto payments usually can't be reversed. Once it's sent, it's gone.

## A favorite of scammers

Because payments are hard to trace and reverse, scammers love crypto. The FTC warns that anyone who insists you pay in crypto is very likely a scammer. Watch out for:

- Promises of sure profits or huge returns
- Strangers online offering to "help you invest"
- Free crypto giveaways that ask you to send some first

## Age rules

Most crypto apps require users to be adults. Kids should never send money or personal details to anyone online about crypto without a trusted grown-up.

This article is for learning. It is not advice about what to buy.

## Try it

With a grown-up, read the FTC page about cryptocurrency scams. List three warning signs. Then compare crypto with a savings account: which is insured, and which can swing in price?
