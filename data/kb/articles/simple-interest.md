Interest is extra money paid for using money. There are two main ways to work it out. The easier one is called **simple interest**.

## The idea

With simple interest, you earn interest only on the money you first put in. That starting amount is called the **principal**. The interest you earn does not earn more interest.

## The formula

Simple interest = principal times rate times time.

- **Principal:** the money you start with.
- **Rate:** the interest rate for one year, as a decimal. For example, 5% is 0.05.
- **Time:** how many years.

## An example

You put $100 somewhere that pays 5% simple interest per year, for 3 years.

- Each year: $100 times 0.05 equals $5.
- Over 3 years: $5 times 3 equals $15.
- At the end you have $100 plus $15, which is $115.

Notice that you earn the same $5 every year. The interest never grows.

## Simple vs compound

With **compound interest**, you also earn interest on your interest. In the example above, compound interest would give a little more than $115, because the $5 from year one would start earning too. Over many years, that difference can get big.

Most savings accounts use compound interest. Simple interest shows up in some loans and is a great first step for understanding how interest works.

## Borrowing works the same way

If you borrow money with simple interest, the same formula tells you how much extra you pay.

## Try it

Work out the simple interest on $200 at 3% for 4 years. Then work out what you would have in total. Check your answer by doing one year at a time.
