Investing has a lot of special words. Here are some of the most common, explained simply.

## ETF

An **ETF**, or exchange-traded fund, holds many investments at once and is bought and sold on a stock exchange like one share.

## Index fund

An **index fund** copies a list of investments called an index, like the S&P 500, so it moves up and down with that part of the market.

## Broker

A **broker** is a company that buys and sells investments for people, usually through a website or app. Kids need a parent to open an account.

## Dividend

A **dividend** is money some companies pay to their shareholders, often from profits. It is not promised and can change.

## Return

A **return** is how much an investment gained or lost, often shown as a percent. A return can be negative.

## Bull market

A **bull market** is a long stretch when prices in the market mostly rise. Think of a bull pushing its horns up.

## Bear market

A **bear market** is a long stretch when prices fall a lot, often by 20% or more from a high. Think of a bear swiping its paws down.

## Ticker symbol

A **ticker symbol** is a short code for a company's shares, like DIS for Walt Disney.

This is for learning. It is not advice about what to buy.
