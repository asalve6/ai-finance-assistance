A budget is a plan for your money. It tells your money where to go before you spend it.

## Why make a plan?

Without a plan, money can slip away. A snack here, a game item there, and soon it is gone. You might not even remember where it went. A budget helps you choose on purpose.

## The two sides of a budget

A budget has two parts.

- **Money coming in.** This is your **income**. For a kid, it might be an allowance, birthday money, or money from chores.
- **Money going out.** This is what you save, spend, and give.

The rule is simple. Money going out should not be more than money coming in.

## A simple kid budget

Say you get $10 a week. You could plan it like this:

- Save: $4, for a goal like a bike
- Spend: $5, for fun things this week
- Give: $1, to help someone else

That adds up to $10. Every dollar has a job.

## Stick to it, and adjust it

A budget is not a cage. If your plan does not work, change it. Maybe you need a little more for spending, or you want to reach your goal faster. Look at your plan each week and ask, "Did this work?"

## Grown-ups budget too

Families make budgets for big things. They plan for rent or a mortgage, food, bills, and savings. Your small budget is practice for that bigger one.

## Try it

Write down all the money you expect to get this month. Then make three columns: save, spend, and give. Decide how much goes in each. At the end of the month, check how close you came to your plan.
