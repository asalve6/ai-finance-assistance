Getting an allowance is one way to have money. Earning it is another, and it teaches a lot about work and value.

## Two kinds of earning

- **Services** are things you do for people. Walking a dog, raking leaves, or watering plants are services.
- **Products** are things you make and sell. Bracelets, drawings, cookies, or lemonade are products.

## Ideas to try

- Extra chores at home, beyond your usual ones
- Helping a neighbor with yard work or pet care
- Washing cars with friends
- Selling crafts or baked goods at a family event
- Selling old toys or books you no longer use, with permission

## Safety comes first

Always get a grown-up's permission before you try to earn money. Only work for people your family knows and trusts. Never go into someone's home without a grown-up's okay. Never share your address or personal details online to find work.

## What earning teaches

When you earn money, you learn what your time is worth. A $20 game feels different when you know it took two afternoons of yard work. You also learn to be reliable, to be polite to customers, and to do a good job so people ask you back.

## Don't forget costs

If you make products, remember that supplies cost money. Your profit is what is left after paying for them.

## Rules for older kids

As kids get older, they may get a real job. There are laws about how old you must be and how many hours you can work. A grown-up can help you find out the rules where you live.

## Try it

Make a list of three ways you could earn money with skills you already have. Talk with a grown-up about which one is safe and possible. Try one, and write down what you earned and what you learned.
