Why do concert tickets for a famous band cost so much? Why are winter coats on sale in spring? The answer is supply and demand.

## Supply: how much there is

**Supply** is how much of something is available to buy. If a store has a hundred of a toy, the supply is large. If it has only two, the supply is small.

## Demand: how much people want it

**Demand** is how many people want to buy something, and how much they are willing to pay. A new game everyone is talking about has high demand. Last year's sweater might have low demand.

## How they push prices

- When demand is high and supply is low, prices tend to rise. Sellers know people will pay more to get one of the few that exist.
- When supply is high and demand is low, prices tend to fall. Sellers lower prices to get people to buy.

Think of a lemonade stand. On a hot day, many people want lemonade. If you are the only stand, you might charge a bit more. On a cold day, few people want it, so a lower price might help you sell.

## Shortages and extra stock

When something sells out and people still want it, that is a **shortage**. When stores have more than people want, they have extra stock. Stores often put that extra stock on sale.

## It works for stocks too

Share prices follow the same idea. When more people want to buy a company's shares than sell them, the price tends to go up. When more people want to sell, it tends to go down.

## Try it

Think of something that was hard to find, like a popular toy or a new game console. Was the price higher than usual? Now think of something on sale at the end of a season. What happened to supply and demand in each case?
