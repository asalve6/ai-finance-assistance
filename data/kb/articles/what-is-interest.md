Interest is the price of using someone else's money. It can work for you, or against you. It depends on which side you are on.

## When you save

When you keep money in a savings account, the bank gets to use it. In return, the bank pays you a little extra. That extra money is interest. It is like a thank-you payment for letting the bank use your money.

## When you borrow

When someone borrows money, they must pay it back. They also pay extra for the chance to use it. That extra is interest too. This time, it costs them money.

So savers earn interest, and borrowers pay interest.

## The interest rate

Interest is usually shown as a percent for one year. This is called the **interest rate**.

Here is an example. Say you save $100 at a rate of 2% a year. After one year, you earn $2 in interest. Now you have $102.

A higher rate means more interest. If the rate were 4%, you would earn $4 on the same $100.

## Interest on interest

Next year, you earn interest on $102, not just $100. You earn interest on your interest too. This is called compound interest. Over many years, it can make savings grow faster.

## Rates change

Banks can change their rates. Different banks may also offer different rates. That is why grown-ups often compare rates before choosing where to save.

## Try it

Pretend you have $50 in a savings account that pays 2% a year. Work out how much interest you would earn in one year. Then try the same thing at 4%. How much more do you earn at the higher rate?
