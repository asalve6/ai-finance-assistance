When people borrow money, a few special words come up again and again. Here they are, explained simply.

## Credit score

A **credit score** is a number that shows how well someone has handled borrowing, like paying bills on time. A higher score can make borrowing easier and cheaper.

## Credit report

A **credit report** is the record behind the score. It lists loans, credit cards, and whether payments were on time.

## APR

**APR** stands for annual percentage rate. It is the yearly cost of borrowing, including interest and some fees, shown as a percent. A lower APR means cheaper borrowing.

## Collateral

**Collateral** is something valuable that backs a loan. For a car loan, the car is the collateral. If the loan isn't paid, the lender can take it.

## Co-signer

A **co-signer** is a second person who promises to pay a loan if the borrower can't. It is a big promise, so co-signers think carefully first.

## Minimum payment

The **minimum payment** is the smallest amount you must pay on a bill each month. Paying only the minimum means the debt lasts longer and costs more in interest.

## Late fee

A **late fee** is a charge for paying after the due date. Late payments can also hurt a credit score.

## Principal

The **principal** is the amount you borrowed, not counting interest.

## Try it

Imagine a $100 debt with a $10 minimum payment. If you pay only $10 a month, how many months until it's gone, not counting interest? Why would interest make it take even longer?
