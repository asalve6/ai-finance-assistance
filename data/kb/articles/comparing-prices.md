Two stores can sell the very same thing at different prices. Comparing before you buy is one of the easiest ways to make your money go further.

## Check more than one place

Before buying something you have saved for, look at a few stores or websites. A grown-up can help you search online. You might find the same game for a few dollars less.

## Compare the size, not just the price

A bigger box usually costs more, but it may be a better deal. To compare fairly, look at the **unit price**. That is the price for one unit, like one ounce or one item.

Here is an example:

- A small pack of 10 pencils costs $2. That is 20 cents per pencil.
- A big pack of 30 pencils costs $4.50. That is 15 cents per pencil.

The big pack costs more in total, but each pencil is cheaper. Many stores show the unit price on the shelf tag.

## A deal is only a deal if you need it

"Buy 2, get 1 free" sounds great. But if you only need one, buying three is not saving money. A sale is only good if it is something you planned to buy anyway.

## Quality matters too

The cheapest choice is not always the best value. A $10 backpack that rips in a month may cost more in the end than a $20 one that lasts for years. Think about how long something will last.

## Watch for extra costs

Shipping, fees, and sales tax can change the total. Compare the final price, not just the sticker price.

## Try it

Next time you go to the store with a grown-up, pick one item. Find two sizes or two brands. Work out the unit price for each. Which is the better value? Would you still choose it?
