Surprises happen. A bike tire pops, a phone screen cracks, or a favorite pair of shoes falls apart. An **emergency fund** is money you keep just for moments like these.

## What makes it different?

Your emergency fund is not for treats or for your savings goal. It sits quietly until something unexpected happens. Some people call it a "rainy day fund."

## Why have one?

Without an emergency fund, a surprise can wreck your plans. You might have to empty your savings jar for your game to fix your bike. Or a grown-up might have to borrow money and pay interest. With an emergency fund, you can fix the problem and keep your other plans on track.

## How much?

For a kid, a small amount is a great start, maybe enough to replace a common thing you use. For grown-ups, some experts suggest saving enough to cover several months of living costs, because losing a job or a big repair can cost a lot. Every family decides what works for them.

## Where to keep it

An emergency fund should be safe and easy to get when you need it. For a kid, that might be a separate envelope or jar. For grown-ups, it is often a savings account. It should not be somewhere where its value goes up and down a lot, because you might need it at a bad time.

## Fill it back up

If you use your emergency fund, that is what it is for. Then start adding to it again, a little at a time.

## Try it

Make a small envelope or jar and label it "Emergency." Decide on a starting goal. Each week, add a small amount until you reach it. Then think of one surprise it could help with.
