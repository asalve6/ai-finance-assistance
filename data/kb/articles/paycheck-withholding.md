Many people feel surprised by their first paycheck. They worked 10 hours at $12 an hour and expected $120. But the check says less. Where did the rest go?

## Gross pay and net pay

- **Gross pay** is the total you earned before anything is taken out. In the example, that is $120.
- **Net pay** is what you actually get. It is sometimes called take-home pay.

The difference is money that was **withheld**. Your employer takes it out of your pay and sends it to the government for you.

## What gets withheld?

- **Federal income tax.** A tax on money you earn. How much depends on how much you earn and other things about you.
- **Social Security.** Money that helps pay benefits to retired people and some people who can't work.
- **Medicare.** Money that helps pay for health care for older people.
- **State and local taxes.** Many states, and some cities, have their own income tax. Some don't.

Social Security and Medicare are often shown together as "FICA" on a pay stub.

## The W-4 form

When you start a job, you fill out a form called a **W-4**. It tells your employer how much federal income tax to withhold. A grown-up can help you fill it out.

## Getting some back

Sometimes too much income tax is withheld during the year. After the year ends, people file a tax return. If too much was withheld, they can get a **refund**. If too little was withheld, they may owe more.

## Read your pay stub

Every paycheck comes with a pay stub. It lists your gross pay, each thing withheld, and your net pay. Checking it helps you understand where your money went.

This is for learning, not tax advice.

## Try it

Ask a grown-up if you can look at an old pay stub with them, with private numbers covered. Find the gross pay, the net pay, and each thing withheld. Add up the withheld amounts. Does gross minus withheld equal net?
