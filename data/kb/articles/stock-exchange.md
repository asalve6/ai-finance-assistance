When people buy and sell shares of companies, they need a place to meet. That place is called a **stock exchange**.

## A marketplace for shares

A stock exchange is like a giant marketplace. Instead of fruit or toys, people trade shares of companies. Buyers say what price they will pay. Sellers say what price they will accept. When a buyer and a seller agree, a trade happens.

## Famous exchanges

In the US, two of the best known are the **New York Stock Exchange**, or NYSE, and **Nasdaq**. Other countries have their own exchanges too. A company's shares are usually listed on one main exchange.

## Mostly online now

Long ago, traders stood on a crowded floor and shouted orders. Today, almost all trading happens on computers, very fast. A single trade can take a tiny fraction of a second.

## How regular people trade

Most people don't trade on an exchange directly. They use a **broker**, a company that places trades for them, usually through a website or app. Kids can't open their own brokerage account. A parent can open a special account for a child, called a custodial account.

## When it is open

US stock exchanges are usually open on weekdays during business hours, Eastern Time. They close on weekends and some holidays. Prices you see when the market is closed are from the last trade.

## Rules keep it fair

In the US, the Securities and Exchange Commission, the SEC, makes rules to keep trading fair and honest. Public companies must share reports about their business, so everyone gets the same key information.
