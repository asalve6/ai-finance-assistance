Would you rather have one cookie now, or two cookies if you wait 15 minutes? Choosing to wait for something better is a skill. It is sometimes called **delayed gratification**, which means waiting for a reward.

## A famous experiment

Many years ago, researchers offered young children a treat. The children could eat one treat right away, or wait a short time and get two. Some ate it fast. Others found clever ways to wait, like covering their eyes or singing a song. Later studies showed that a child's situation matters too. For example, kids are more likely to wait when they trust the reward will really come.

## Why waiting helps with money

Saving is a kind of waiting. When you save your allowance instead of spending it, you are choosing a bigger reward later, like a bike, over a small one now.

## Tricks for waiting

- **Keep the goal in sight.** A picture of your goal near your jar reminds you why you are waiting.
- **Keep the money out of sight.** If it is harder to grab, it is easier not to spend.
- **Break it into steps.** Celebrate each week you reach your saving target.
- **Do something else.** When you feel like spending, find a fun activity that is free.

## Waiting is not always the answer

Sometimes now is the right time, like buying a birthday gift before the party. Waiting is a tool, not a rule. Use it when a bigger goal matters more.

## Try it

Pick something small you want this week. Wait three days before buying it. Write down how you feel each day. After three days, do you still want it? Was the waiting easier than you thought?
