How big is a company? You could count its workers or its stores. Investors often use a different measure: **market capitalization**, or **market cap** for short.

## The formula

Market cap = share price × number of shares.

Here is a made-up example. A company has 10 million shares, and each share costs $50.

10,000,000 × $50 = $500,000,000

So its market cap is $500 million. That is roughly what it would cost to buy every share at today's price.

## A share price alone can fool you

A $500 share does not mean a bigger company than a $50 share. It depends on how many shares there are. A company with a $50 share and a billion shares is worth far more than one with a $500 share and a million shares. Market cap compares companies fairly.

## Big, medium, and small

People sort companies by market cap:

- **Large cap:** very big, well-known companies
- **Mid cap:** medium-sized companies
- **Small cap:** smaller companies

Smaller companies can grow faster, but their prices often swing more. Larger companies are often steadier, but not always.

## It changes every day

Because share prices move, market cap moves too. A company's market cap can grow or shrink by a lot in a single day.

## What it doesn't tell you

Market cap is not the same as how much money a company makes, or whether it is a good company. A company can be worth a lot and still lose money for years.

This article is for learning. It is not advice about what to buy.

## Try it

A company has 2 million shares at $30 each. Another has 500,000 shares at $100 each. Work out both market caps. Which company is bigger? Did the share price alone tell you the answer?
