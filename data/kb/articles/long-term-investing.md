If you planted a tree, you wouldn't dig it up every day to check the roots. You'd water it and wait. Thinking long term about money works a bit like that.

## Days vs years

Prices of investments like stocks go up and down every day. Over a single day or week, the moves can look random and even scary. Over many years, those small bumps blur into a bigger picture.

The length of time before you need your money is called your **time horizon**. A long time horizon gives more room to wait out bad stretches.

## What history shows, and doesn't

Over long stretches of the past, the US stock market as a whole has grown, with many big drops along the way. Some drops took years to recover from. The past does not promise the future, and some single companies never recover at all.

## Why long-term thinking helps

- **Less stress.** Not reacting to every daily move.
- **Fewer rushed choices.** Big decisions made in a panic are often regretted.
- **Time to grow.** Compound growth needs years to do its work.

## Steady habits

Long-term thinking pairs well with steady habits, like adding a little money on a regular schedule. Just like saving drop by drop, small amounts added over time can matter more than trying to pick the perfect moment.

## Long term is not "never look"

Thinking long term doesn't mean ignoring things. It means checking in calmly, maybe a few times a year, and asking if your plan still fits your goals.

## Try it

In Penny's practice portfolio, write down today's total value. Don't change anything for one month, then check again. Note the biggest daily change you saw along the way, and compare it to the change over the whole month.
