People pay taxes in many ways: on what they earn, what they buy, and the homes they own. But where does all that money go?

## Paying for things we share

Taxes pay for things that everyone in a community shares. Most people could not pay for these alone, but together it works.

Look around your day:

- **Your school.** Teachers, buses, and books are often paid for with local and state taxes.
- **Roads and bridges.** The street outside your home was built and fixed with tax money.
- **Parks and libraries.** Free to visit, because taxes help pay for them.
- **Firefighters and police.** They keep communities safe.
- **Clean water.** Many places use tax money to keep water safe to drink.

## Help for people who need it

Some tax money helps people directly. Programs help older people, people with disabilities, and families who are going through hard times.

## Different levels of government

- **Local** governments, like your city or county, often pay for schools, parks, and police.
- **State** governments often pay for highways, state colleges, and more.
- The **federal** government, the national one, pays for things like the military, national parks, and big programs for older people.

## Who decides?

In a democracy, the people voters elect decide how tax money is spent. They argue, debate, and vote on budgets. That is one reason voting matters.

## Try it

Go on a "tax walk" with a grown-up around your neighborhood. Count how many things you see that tax money helps pay for. Make a list, then guess which level of government pays for each one.
