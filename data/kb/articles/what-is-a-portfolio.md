Artists keep a portfolio, a folder of their best work. Investors have one too. An investment **portfolio** is everything a person has invested in, looked at as one group.

## What is in a portfolio?

Each thing in a portfolio is called a **holding**. A holding might be shares of a company, a fund, or other investments. For example, a portfolio could hold 3 shares of Walt Disney and 5 shares of Coca-Cola.

## What is it worth?

To find a portfolio's value, multiply each holding's shares by its price, then add them up.

Say one Disney share costs $110 and one Coca-Cola share costs $70. Then:

- 3 Disney shares: 3 times $110 equals $330
- 5 Coca-Cola shares: 5 times $70 equals $350
- Total: $680

Because prices change every day, the value of a portfolio changes too.

## Looking at the whole picture

The power of a portfolio is seeing everything together. You can ask:

- Which holding is the biggest part?
- Is my money spread out or mostly in one place?
- How bumpy are the prices overall?

Penny's Portfolio tab answers these questions for a practice portfolio, using real prices.

## Practice first

Real investing uses real money, and prices can go down as well as up. A practice portfolio lets you learn how it all works without risking anything. You can try changes and see what happens.

This article is for learning. It is not advice about what to buy.
