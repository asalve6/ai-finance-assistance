Some share prices move gently, like a calm lake. Others jump around like a bouncy ride. The word for this is **volatility**.

## What volatility means

Volatility describes how much a price moves up and down, and how fast. A price that often moves 3% in a day is more volatile than one that usually moves less than 1%.

Penny calls this "bumpiness." Its Portfolio tab labels a practice portfolio as calmer, medium, or bumpier, based on the typical daily move.

## How it is measured

One common way is to look at the daily percentage changes over a period, like 30 trading days. Then you measure how spread out those changes are. That measure is called the **standard deviation**. A bigger number means the daily moves are larger and less predictable.

You don't need the formula to get the idea. If the line on a price chart looks like a jagged mountain range, it is volatile. If it looks like gentle hills, it is calmer.

## Why it matters

Volatility is one way to think about risk. A very bumpy price could be much lower on the day you need your money. A calmer price usually swings less, though it can still fall.

Volatility also works upward. A bumpy price can rise quickly too. That is part of the trade-off between risk and reward.

## Volatility and time

Over one day, prices can swing a lot. Over many years, the daily bumps matter less to people who don't need their money soon. That is why people investing for the long term may care less about short-term bumps.

## Spread helps

A portfolio of several companies is often less bumpy than one company alone, because not all of them move the same way on the same day.
