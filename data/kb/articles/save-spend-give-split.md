One of the simplest money plans uses three jars: **save**, **spend**, and **give**. Every time you get money, you split it among them.

## What each jar is for

- **Save:** money for goals and for later, like a bike or an emergency.
- **Spend:** money you can use now, on things you need or enjoy.
- **Give:** money to help others or a cause you care about.

## Choosing a split

There is no single right split. Here is one example for every $10:

- Save: $5 (50%)
- Spend: $4 (40%)
- Give: $1 (10%)

Some families save more, some give more. What matters is choosing your split on purpose and sticking to it.

## Using percents

Percents make splitting easy, no matter how much money you get. If your split is 50% save, 40% spend, and 10% give, then:

- With $20 of birthday money: save $10, spend $8, give $2.
- With $6 from chores: save $3, spend $2.40, give 60 cents.

## Why three jars help

When money sits in one pile, it is easy to spend it all. Separate jars make each job clear. Your spend jar can run out without touching your savings.

## Change it as you grow

When you have a big goal, you might save more for a while. As you get older and have more expenses, your split may change. Look at it every few months and ask if it still fits.

## Try it

Set up three jars or envelopes and label them save, spend, and give. Decide your split as percents. Next time you get money, divide it right away. After a month, check: did the split feel right?
