Have you ever bought something, then wished you had not? Many people have. Often it was an **impulse buy**.

## What is an impulse buy?

An impulse buy is something you buy on the spot, without planning. You see it, you want it, and you buy it right away.

Stores know this. That is why candy and small toys sit right by the checkout. Games and apps do it too, with flashing offers like "Only 1 hour left!" These tricks are made to make you buy fast.

## Why it matters

Small impulse buys add up. A $2 snack three times a week is $6. Over a month, that is about $24. That money could have gone toward something you really wanted.

Also, things bought in a rush often stop feeling special soon after.

## The 24-hour rule

Here is a simple trick. When you want to buy something you did not plan for, wait 24 hours. One whole day.

After a day, ask yourself:

- Do I still want it?
- Is it a need or a want?
- Will I still use it next month?
- Is there something I want more that this money could help with?

Often, the feeling fades. If you still want it after a day, and it fits your budget, then buying it is a real choice, not an impulse.

## Not all quick buys are bad

Sometimes buying right away is fine, like a cold drink on a hot day. The goal is not to never have fun. It is to make sure you decide, not the store.

## Try it

For one week, every time you want to buy something unplanned, write it down instead. Wait one day. At the end of the week, count how many things you still wanted. How much money did you keep?
