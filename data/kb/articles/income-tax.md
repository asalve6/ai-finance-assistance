When people earn money from a job or a business, part of it goes to the government. That part is called **income tax**.

## Who collects it?

In the US, the federal government collects income tax through the **IRS**, the Internal Revenue Service. Most states, and some cities, collect their own income tax too. A few states have no state income tax.

## Taxes in steps

The federal income tax uses **tax brackets**. Income is split into layers. The first layer is taxed at a low rate. The next layer is taxed a bit higher, and so on.

Here is a made-up example with simple numbers:

- The first $10 of each $100 earned: taxed at 10%
- The rest: taxed at 20%

Someone earning $100 pays 10% on the first $10 (that is $1) and 20% on the other $90 (that is $18). Their total tax is $19, not $20. Moving into a higher bracket only raises the tax on the money in that higher layer.

Real brackets use much bigger numbers, and they change over time.

## Paying during the year

Most workers pay income tax a little at a time. Their employer withholds some from each paycheck and sends it to the government.

## The tax return

After the year ends, many people fill out a **tax return**. It adds up their income and their tax. In the US, it is usually due in mid-April. If too much was withheld, they get a refund. If too little, they pay the rest.

## What it pays for

Income tax helps pay for things like national defense, roads, health programs, and many other government services.
